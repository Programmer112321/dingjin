Radar Datasets

Microwave radar remote sensing datasets for soil moisture, vegetation structure, and surface deformation monitoring.

Sentinel-1 — ESA Copernicus

Sentinel-1 provides C-band Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) imagery, enabling cloud-penetrating, day-and-night observations of land and ocean surfaces.

Agency: European Space Agency (ESA) — Copernicus Programme Sensor: C-band SAR (5.405 GHz · 5.55 cm wavelength) Spatial resolution: ~5 × 20 m single-look resolution (~10 m processed GRD products) Temporal resolution: 6–12 days depending on satellite availability and latitude Common polarizations: VV+VH (most land observations) or HH+HV depending on acquisition mode and region Key applications: Forest above-ground biomass, flood mapping, soil moisture, ice monitoring, subsidence detection

Note

Sentinel-1 data are freely available through the Copernicus Data Space and Google Earth Engine.

SMAP — Soil Moisture Active Passive (NASA)

SMAP is a NASA satellite mission designed to measure the moisture content of Earth’s soil surface globally and repeatedly. Its L-band radiometer provides one of the most widely validated global soil moisture products available from space, and is widely used in hydrology, agriculture, climate modeling, and ecosystem studies.

Agency: NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) Sensor: L-band passive radiometer (1.41 GHz · 21.3 cm wavelength); active radar ceased operation in July 2015 Spatial resolution: ~36 km (radiometer) · 9 km (enhanced product) Temporal resolution: 2–3 day global revisit Launch: January 2015 Key products: - SPL3SMP — Daily global soil moisture (36 km) - SPL3SMP_E — Enhanced daily soil moisture (9 km) - SPL4SMGP — Surface and root-zone soil moisture (9 km, model-assimilated) Key applications: Drought monitoring, flood forecasting, agricultural water use, carbon flux modeling, forest moisture stress

Note

SMAP data are freely available through NASA EarthData and Google Earth Engine.

ALOS-2 PALSAR-2 — JAXA

ALOS-2 PALSAR-2 is a Japanese L-band SAR satellite operated by JAXA. L-band radar penetrates forest canopy more effectively than C-band, making it particularly valuable for above-ground biomass estimation and forest structure retrieval.

Agency: Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) Sensor: L-band SAR (1.2575 GHz · 23.8 cm wavelength) Spatial resolution: 3 m (Spotlight) · 6–10 m (Stripmap) · 60–100 m (ScanSAR), mode dependent Temporal resolution: 14-day repeat cycle Polarizations: Single-, dual-, and quad-polarization modes (HH, HV, VV, VH depending on acquisition mode) Key applications: Forest biomass estimation, forest/non-forest mapping, disaster monitoring, wetland mapping

Note

ALOS-2 PALSAR-2 global mosaic data are freely available through JAXA EORC.

TanDEM-X — DLR

TanDEM-X is a German radar mission that produced a highly accurate global Digital Elevation Model (DEM) using X-band bistatic InSAR, flying in close formation with TerraSAR-X. The global DEM is widely used in canopy height estimation workflows and as a terrain correction layer.

Agency: German Aerospace Center (DLR) Sensor: X-band SAR (9.65 GHz · 3.1 cm wavelength), bistatic InSAR Spatial resolution: 12 m (global DEM posting) Vertical accuracy: Relative accuracy ~2 m (flat open terrain); absolute accuracy typically <10 m globally Key applications: Global DEM generation, canopy height estimation workflows, glacier monitoring, terrain analysis

Note

TanDEM-X global DEM data are available through DLR.

NISAR — NASA / ISRO (Initial operations 2025)

NISAR (NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar) is a joint Earth observation mission between NASA and the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). It is the first spaceborne SAR mission combining fully operational L-band and S-band imaging radars, designed to measure ecosystem disturbances, ice-sheet dynamics, natural hazards, and sea-level change. For forest research, NISAR’s L-band radar is expected to provide global above-ground biomass and forest disturbance data at high spatial resolution and frequent revisit.

Agency: NASA (Jet Propulsion Laboratory) + ISRO Launch: March 2025; initial science operations beginning 2025 Sensors: L-band SAR (1.25 GHz · 24.0 cm wavelength, NASA) + S-band SAR (3.2 GHz · 9.4 cm wavelength, ISRO) Spatial resolution: 3–10 m depending on observation mode Temporal resolution: 12-day repeat cycle Swath width: 240 km Key applications: Forest above-ground biomass, deforestation and disturbance mapping, wetland dynamics, earthquake and landslide monitoring, glacier flow, sea-level change

Note

NISAR data will be freely available through NASA EarthData and ISRO’s NRSC portal.

BIOMASS — ESA (Launched April 2025)

BIOMASS is ESA’s first P-band SAR satellite, specifically designed to produce the first global maps of forest above-ground biomass. P-band radar (~69 cm wavelength) penetrates deep into forest canopy and is sensitive to the woody structure of trunks and large branches — the components that store most carbon in forests. BIOMASS is a state-of-the-art mission for global carbon cycle science and is expected to directly support international carbon accounting and climate policy.

Agency: European Space Agency (ESA) — Earth Explorer Mission Launch: April 2025 Sensor: P-band SAR (435 MHz · 69 cm wavelength), fully polarimetric Spatial resolution: ~50–200 m (standard biomass products); finer native SAR image resolution available Temporal resolution: ~25-day repeat cycle; multiple observation passes used for tomographic forest structure retrieval Mission phases: - Tomographic phase (Years 1–3): 3D forest structure mapping using SAR tomography - Interferometric phase (Years 4–5): Change detection and biomass updating Key products: Global forest above-ground biomass density, forest height, forest disturbance Key applications: Forest carbon stock estimation, deforestation monitoring, carbon flux modeling, climate change assessment

Note

BIOMASS data will be freely available through ESA’s Earth Online portal.

CYGNSS — Cyclone Global Navigation Satellite System (NASA)

CYGNSS is a constellation of eight microsatellites that use GPS signals reflected off Earth’s surface (GNSS-R) to measure wind speed over the ocean and soil moisture over land. Unlike traditional active radar satellites, CYGNSS is a passive bistatic radar system that exploits signals of opportunity from GPS — enabling rapid, global coverage at low cost.

Agency: NASA Goddard Space Flight Center Launched: December 2016 Technique: GNSS-Reflectometry (GNSS-R) — passive bistatic radar using reflected GPS L1 (1.575 GHz · 19.0 cm wavelength) signals Spatial resolution: ~25 km (nominal ocean wind product) · varies for land soil moisture retrievals Temporal resolution: Median revisit ~7 hours globally; ~2-day revisit at mid-latitudes Coverage: ±35° latitude (tropical belt) Key products: - CYGNSS L2 — Ocean surface wind speed - CYGNSS L3 — Gridded wind speed and soil moisture (25 km) Key applications: Tropical cyclone intensity monitoring, soil moisture in densely vegetated tropical forests (regions where L-band retrievals are attenuated), inland flood inundation, wetland dynamics

Note

CYGNSS data are freely available through NASA EarthData (Physical Oceanography DAAC, PO.DAAC) and Google Earth Engine.

GRACE-FO — Gravity Recovery and Climate Experiment Follow-On (NASA / GFZ)

GRACE-FO (and its predecessor GRACE, 2002–2017) measures subtle changes in Earth’s gravity field caused by large-scale mass redistribution — primarily changes in water storage. By tracking how the gravity field changes month to month, GRACE-FO provides unique observations of groundwater depletion, ice sheet mass loss, ocean mass change, and total water storage anomalies that are impossible to obtain from any other remote sensing approach.

Agency: NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) / GFZ German Research Centre for Geosciences Launched: May 2018 (GRACE-FO); predecessor GRACE operated 2002–2017 Technique: Dual-satellite microwave ranging (K/Ka-band, ~26 GHz) combined with GPS and accelerometers; not an imaging radar — gravity field inversion Spatial resolution: ~300 km (spherical harmonic truncation at degree/order 60–96) Temporal resolution: Monthly global gravity field solutions; some mascon products provide 10-day updates Key products: - GRACE-FO RL06 — Monthly gravity field coefficients (CSR, JPL, GFZ) - JPL Mascon — Terrestrial Water Storage (TWS) anomalies on equal-area mascons - GSFC Mascon — High-resolution TWS solution Key applications: Groundwater depletion (aquifer monitoring), ice sheet and glacier mass balance, drought quantification, seasonal water cycle, sea level rise attribution, post-glacial rebound

Tip

GRACE/GRACE-FO is the only spaceborne tool capable of directly detecting groundwater changes at continental scale, making it uniquely valuable for drought early warning and water resource management in data-sparse regions.

Note

GRACE-FO data are freely available through NASA EarthData (PODAAC), JPL GRACE Tellus, and Google Earth Engine (NASA/GRACE/MASS_GRIDS).

Sentinel-6 — Copernicus (ESA / EUMETSAT / NASA)

Sentinel-6 Michael Freilich is a radar altimetry mission dedicated to measuring global sea surface height with millimeter-level precision. While primarily an oceanographic mission, its altimetric data also support inland water body monitoring (lake levels, river stages) and ice sheet surface elevation change.

Agency: ESA / EUMETSAT / European Commission Copernicus Programme; in partnership with NASA and NOAA Launched: November 2020 Sensor: Poseidon-4 dual-frequency radar altimeter (Ku-band: 13.575 GHz · 2.2 cm; C-band: 5.41 GHz · 5.5 cm) Orbital altitude: 1336 km, 10-day exact repeat cycle Spatial coverage: ±66° latitude Measurement accuracy: ~2–3 cm sea surface height precision Key products: - L2 Ocean Products — Significant wave height, wind speed, sea surface height anomaly - L2 Inland Water — Lake and reservoir water levels Key applications: Global sea level rise monitoring, ocean circulation, El Niño/La Niña dynamics, lake and reservoir water level changes, ice sheet surface height trends

Note

Sentinel-6 data are freely available through the Copernicus Data Space and EUMETSAT Data Store.
